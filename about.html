01 — Our Story

Told in three chapters.

Chapter 01 · The Founding

It began with a question.

Allegiance Academy was founded on a simple refusal — the refusal to treat schooling as storage. One building, a handful of teachers, and a conviction that children learn best when they are trusted with real questions, real responsibility and real work.

Est. — [Year]The founding cohort

Chapter 02 · The Practice

A campus in full practice.

Today the academy runs on rhythm. Four houses that compete and care for each other, studios that stay open past the last bell, laboratories that expect mistakes, and a timetable deliberately built to leave room to think.

Four housesDay & residential

Chapter 03 · The Direction

Built for what comes next.

The next chapter is already under construction — a centre for science and innovation, greener grounds, and a growing community of alumni who keep coming back, not to visit, but to teach what they have learned.

02 — Leadership

Guided by principled hands.

Two people set the tone for everything that happens here — and it begins with the student standing in front of them.

06 — Values

What we stand on.

Five words, chosen slowly and kept seriously. Hover or tap each one to see what it costs.

  • Questions are treated as beginnings, not interruptions. A student who asks why is given room to chase it — down a library aisle, into a laboratory, all the way to an answer they can defend.

  • The curriculum behind the curriculum — practised in how we keep score, share credit, own our mistakes and treat the person nobody is watching us treat.

  • The nerve to raise a hand, defend an unpopular answer, audition anyway and fail in front of people. We stage that moment weekly.

  • Not a room on this campus but a habit across it — an equation arrived at sideways, a poem in a science notebook, a better way to run the tuck shop.

  • Four houses, many clubs, one school. Belonging is engineered on purpose: nobody eats alone, nobody learns alone, and everybody carries something for someone else.
